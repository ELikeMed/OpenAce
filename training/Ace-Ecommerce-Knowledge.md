# Ace Ecommerce Knowledge Base
## Selling Products Online

This volume covers platform choice, product pages, checkout and cart abandonment, shipping and
returns, fulfilment, and the economics of selling physical and digital goods online.

# SECTION 62: CHOOSING A PLATFORM

## The Main Options

**Shopify** — hosted, maintained for you, strong app ecosystem, reliable checkout. Monthly fee
plus transaction costs, and you work within its structure. The default choice for most people
selling physical products who want to sell rather than administer software.

**WooCommerce** — a plugin on top of WordPress. Free to install, enormously flexible, complete
control of the data. You own hosting, security, updates and the consequences of a plugin
conflict. Right when you already run WordPress or need customisation Shopify resists.

**Squarespace, Wix and similar** — simplest to launch, best when the store is a small part of a
mostly-content site. Least flexible as catalogue and volume grow.

**Marketplaces (Amazon, Etsy, eBay)** — built-in demand, which is the hard part. You pay
substantial fees, compete on price beside near-identical listings, and never own the customer
relationship. Excellent for validating demand and for reach; dangerous as the only channel,
because the platform can change fees or rules and you have no direct line to your buyers.

**Custom build** — justified only when the catalogue, pricing logic, or integration genuinely
cannot be expressed on a platform. Checkout is one of the least forgiving things to build: it
handles money, fraud, tax and compliance, and platforms have spent years on the edge cases.

**A practical sequence:** start on a hosted platform, add a marketplace for reach, and build
custom only when a specific limit is demonstrably costing you money.

## Digital Products and Subscriptions

Digital goods avoid inventory, shipping and returns, but bring their own requirements: secure
delivery, licence handling, VAT and sales tax rules that often differ from physical goods, and
higher chargeback rates.

**Subscription commerce** — recurring revenue, higher lifetime value, and a churn problem that
becomes the whole business. Failed payments are a large share of subscription loss and are
recoverable with retry logic and dunning emails; ignore them and you lose customers who never
intended to leave.

# SECTION 63: PRODUCT PAGES AND CONVERSION

## What a Product Page Needs

Multiple photographs from several angles, including scale and in-use context. A clear title and
price. Short scannable benefits before long specifications. Complete details — dimensions,
materials, compatibility, what is in the box. Delivery cost and timescale visible *before*
checkout. Return terms. Reviews. Stock status. One prominent add-to-cart.

**Photography is the highest-return investment on a product page.** Customers cannot handle the
item, so the images carry the entire job of the physical showroom. Poor photographs suppress
sales more than almost any other single factor.

**Write for the person deciding, not the search engine.** Answer the question they are actually
weighing: will this fit, will it last, will it arrive in time, what if it is wrong.

**Show delivery cost early.** Unexpected shipping cost at checkout is the single most cited
reason for abandonment, and hiding it until the last step is what causes it.

**Reviews matter more than copy.** Ask every buyer, publish the mediocre ones too — a perfect
record reads as filtered, and specific criticism with a good response builds more trust than
uniform praise.

## Cart Abandonment

Most shopping carts are abandoned. The causes are predictable and largely fixable.

**The main reasons, roughly in order:** unexpected extra cost at checkout; being forced to create
an account; a long or confusing checkout; concern about payment security; slow delivery; unclear
return policy; and site errors or slowness.

**The fixes follow directly.** Show total cost including shipping and tax as early as possible.
Offer guest checkout — mandatory account creation is pure friction, and you can invite them to
save details after the purchase. Reduce the number of steps and fields, use address
autocomplete, and show a progress indicator. Display trust signals near the payment step: secure
checkout badges, the return policy, and contact details. Offer the payment methods your customers
actually use, including the wallet options that remove typing entirely.

**Abandoned cart emails recover a meaningful share** — typically a first reminder within a few
hours, then one or two follow-ups. Lead with the item and a link straight back to the filled
cart. Discount only in the last message, if at all, or you will train people to abandon
deliberately.

**Fix the checkout before buying more traffic.** Improving checkout conversion raises the return
on every channel simultaneously.

# SECTION 64: SHIPPING, RETURNS AND FULFILMENT

## Shipping

**The options:** free shipping (absorbed into price), flat rate, live carrier rates, free above
a threshold, or local delivery and pickup.

**Free shipping above a threshold is usually the strongest structure** — it removes the
abandonment trigger while protecting margin on small orders, and it raises average order value
because customers add items to reach it. Set the threshold above your current average order.

**"Free" shipping is priced into the product.** Decide deliberately whether your market compares
on item price or on total, because that determines which structure wins.

**Get the packaging right.** Dimensional weight means a large light box can cost more than a
small heavy one, and damage in transit costs the item plus the replacement plus the goodwill.

**Set delivery expectations conservatively and communicate proactively.** Tracking information
and a heads-up about a delay prevent most "where is my order" contacts, which are otherwise a
large share of support volume.

## Returns

**A clear, generous return policy increases sales more than it costs in returns.** It removes the
risk that stops people buying. Hiding or complicating the policy suppresses purchases from
exactly the cautious customers you want.

**State plainly:** the window, the condition required, who pays return shipping, whether it is
refund or exchange or credit, and how long a refund takes.

**Make the process self-service** — a returns portal costs far less than handling each one by
email.

**Track return reasons.** They are free product feedback: a spike in "not as described" is a
photography or copy problem, and a spike in "wrong size" is a sizing guide problem. Fixing the
cause reduces returns permanently.

**Some return rate is normal and healthy.** A rate near zero usually means the policy is
discouraging purchases, not that the product is perfect.

## Fulfilment and Inventory

**In-house** — cheapest at low volume, full control, and it consumes your time. **Third-party
logistics** — a provider stores and ships for you, freeing time at a per-order cost, sensible
once volume makes packing a daily job. **Dropshipping** — no inventory and no capital, but thin
margins, no control over quality or delivery time, and you carry the customer relationship for a
fulfilment process you do not run.

**Inventory ties up cash.** Every unit on a shelf is money you cannot spend elsewhere, which is
why the cash conversion cycle matters more in retail than in most businesses.

**Watch stockouts and dead stock at both ends.** A stockout is lost revenue and a customer who
buys elsewhere; dead stock is capital slowly becoming worthless. Track which lines actually turn,
and discount the ones that do not while they still have value.

## Ecommerce Economics

**Know your contribution per order:** price, minus cost of goods, minus shipping and packaging,
minus payment fees, minus the advertising spent to acquire it. That number, not revenue, tells
you whether growth helps.

**Average order value, conversion rate and repeat purchase rate are the three levers.** Raising
average order value through bundles and thresholds is usually the cheapest of the three, and
repeat purchase is where the profit actually lives, since the second sale carries no acquisition
cost.

**Advertising costs rise as you scale.** Model your economics at double your current acquisition
cost before committing to a growth plan built on paid traffic.
