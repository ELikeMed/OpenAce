# SECTION 1: SALES

## Cold Calling Techniques

The best cold calls follow a 30-second framework: identify yourself, state why you're calling, ask a qualifying question. Never pitch in the first call — your only goal is to book a meeting.

**Opening script:** "Hi [Name], this is [You] from [Company]. I work with [their industry] businesses in [their city] to help them [specific result]. I'm not sure if this is relevant for you — do you have 30 seconds?"

**Key metrics:** Average connect rate is 3-5%. You need 50-80 dials to get 3-4 conversations. Best calling times: Tuesday-Thursday, 10-11:30am and 2-4pm. Monday mornings and Friday afternoons are dead.

**Voicemail script (under 20 seconds):** "Hi [Name], [You] from [Company]. We helped [similar company] increase their [result] by [number]. Wanted to see if that's relevant for you. My number is [number]. Again, [number]."

**Follow-up cadence after cold call:**
- Day 1: Call + voicemail
- Day 2: Email referencing the call
- Day 4: LinkedIn connection request
- Day 7: Second call attempt
- Day 10: Breakup email ("Seems like timing isn't right...")

## Cold Email Outreach

Cold emails have a 1-3% reply rate when done right. Most fail because they're too long, too generic, or too salesy.

**The 3-sentence formula:**
1. One sentence showing you know their business (personalization)
2. One sentence about the result you deliver (value)
3. One sentence asking a question (CTA)

**Example:** "Saw your Google reviews mention wait times — that's costing you roughly 15% of potential customers. We helped [similar business] cut wait times by 40% using automated scheduling. Worth a quick chat?"

**Subject lines that work (30-45% open rate):**
- "Quick question about [their company]"
- "[Mutual connection] suggested I reach out"
- "Idea for [their specific challenge]"
- "[Their competitor] is doing this"

**Follow-up sequence:**
- Email 1: Day 0 (initial outreach)
- Email 2: Day 3 (different angle, same ask)
- Email 3: Day 7 (case study or social proof)
- Email 4: Day 14 (breakup — "Should I close your file?")

Breakup emails get the highest reply rate (25-40%) because of loss aversion.

## Lead Qualification — BANT Framework

BANT: Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline. Ask these early to avoid wasting time on bad leads.

**Budget:** "What kind of budget do you typically set aside for [this type of solution]?" If they say "we don't have a budget," they're not a real lead.

**Authority:** "Who else would be involved in this decision?" If they can't make the decision alone, you need to get to the decision-maker.

**Need:** "What's the biggest challenge you're facing with [problem area] right now?" If they don't have a clear pain, they won't buy.

**Timeline:** "When are you looking to have a solution in place?" If the answer is "someday" or "no rush," they're not buying soon.

**Lead scoring:** Hot (BANT all yes) = follow up daily. Warm (2-3 yes) = follow up weekly. Cold (0-1 yes) = nurture with content, check back in 90 days.

## Objection Handling

**Top 10 objections and responses:**

1. "It's too expensive" → "I understand. Let me ask — what would it cost you to NOT solve this problem over the next 12 months?"

2. "I need to think about it" → "Of course. What specifically do you want to think through? I might be able to help with that right now."

3. "I'm happy with my current provider" → "That's great. What would need to change for you to consider alternatives?"

4. "Send me some information" → "Happy to. So I send you the right thing — what's the main problem you're trying to solve?"

5. "We don't have the budget" → "Understood. If budget weren't a factor, would this be something you'd move forward with?"

6. "I need to talk to my partner/boss" → "Makes sense. Would it help if I joined that conversation to answer any questions they might have?"

7. "Now isn't a good time" → "When would be better? I'll call you then." (Book the specific callback.)

8. "We tried something like this before and it didn't work" → "What happened? I'd love to understand so I can make sure we avoid the same issues."

9. "I can do this myself" → "You absolutely can. The question is whether your time is better spent on this or on running your business."

10. "Your competitor offers the same thing cheaper" → "They might. What matters more — the price or the result? Let me show you our results compared to theirs."

## Follow-Up Strategy

80% of sales happen between the 5th and 12th contact. But 92% of salespeople give up after 4 attempts.

**Optimal cadence:**
- Day 1: Initial contact (call, email, or LinkedIn)
- Day 2: Follow-up via different channel
- Day 5: Value-add (article, case study, tip)
- Day 8: Direct follow-up
- Day 12: Social proof (testimonial, result)
- Day 15: Check-in
- Day 21: Breakup message

**Channel mix:** Don't use just email. Combine email + call + LinkedIn + text. Multi-channel outreach gets 3x the response rate of single-channel.

**Speed to lead:** Respond to inbound leads within 5 minutes. After 30 minutes, your odds of qualifying the lead drop by 21x.

## Closing Techniques

**The Assumptive Close:** Act as if they've already decided. "Should we start on Monday or would Wednesday work better for your team?"

**The Summary Close:** Recap everything they said they need, then ask: "Based on everything we discussed, it sounds like this checks all your boxes. Ready to move forward?"

**The Urgency Close:** Only use when there's real urgency. "This pricing is available through Friday. After that, it goes up 15%."

**The Trial Close:** Test readiness throughout the conversation. "If we could solve [their problem], would you want to move forward?"

**Key principle:** The best close is asking for the sale. Most salespeople never do. "Based on what we've discussed, I think we can really help you. Want to get started?"

## Pipeline Management

**Pipeline stages:**
1. New Lead (just entered the system)
2. Contacted (first outreach sent)
3. Qualified (BANT confirmed)
4. Proposal Sent (pricing/scope delivered)
5. Negotiation (discussing terms)
6. Closed Won / Closed Lost

**Pipeline health metrics:**
- Number of deals per stage
- Average deal size
- Win rate (should be 20-30%)
- Average sales cycle length
- Pipeline velocity (deals × win rate × deal size / cycle length)

**Weekly pipeline review:** Every Monday, review every deal. Remove dead ones. Identify stuck deals. Focus on deals most likely to close this month.

# SECTION 2: MARKETING

## SEO for Small Businesses

Local SEO drives 46% of all Google searches. Three things matter most:

1. **Google Business Profile** — Complete every field. Post weekly. Respond to every review. Add photos monthly. Businesses with 100+ photos get 520% more calls.

2. **Website pages** — Create a page for every service + city combination. "Plumbing Services in Miami" as its own page. Each page should be 800+ words with your city mentioned 3-5 times.

3. **Reviews** — Businesses with 50+ Google reviews rank significantly higher. Ask every happy customer. Text them the direct review link.

**Quick wins:** Claim your Google Business Profile. Add your business to Yelp, BBB, and industry directories. Make sure your name, address, and phone (NAP) are identical everywhere.

## Social Media Strategy by Platform

**LinkedIn (B2B, professional services, consulting):**
- Post 3-5x per week
- Content: industry insights, wins, lessons learned, hot takes
- Engage on prospects' posts BEFORE you pitch them
- Best times: Tuesday-Thursday 8-10am

**Instagram (visual businesses — restaurants, fitness, beauty, real estate):**
- Post daily, Stories 3-5x per day
- Content: before/after, behind-the-scenes, client results, team culture
- Use 20-30 hashtags, mix of popular and niche
- Reels get 2x the reach of static posts

**Facebook (local businesses, community-focused):**
- Join local groups and be helpful (not salesy)
- Run targeted ads to your zip code
- Best for: restaurants, home services, events, retail

**TikTok (reaching younger demographics, any visual business):**
- Post 1-3x per day
- First 3 seconds determine if people watch
- Trending sounds boost reach 10x
- Show the process, not just the result

## Email Marketing

Email generates $36 for every $1 spent — highest ROI of any marketing channel.

**List building:** Offer something valuable (guide, checklist, discount) in exchange for an email. Put the opt-in on every page of your website.

**Email types:**
- Welcome sequence (3 emails over 7 days after signup)
- Weekly newsletter (value, not just promotions)
- Promotional (max 1x per month, tied to a real offer)
- Re-engagement (for subscribers who haven't opened in 90 days)

**Benchmarks:** Open rate above 25% is good. Click rate above 3% is good. Unsubscribe rate below 0.5% is healthy.

## Google Ads

Google Ads captures demand that already exists — someone is searching for what you sell RIGHT NOW.

**When to use:** High-intent services (plumber, lawyer, dentist, contractor). NOT for awareness or brand building — use social for that.

**Budget:** Start with $500-1000/month. Target your city only. Focus on your highest-margin service. One campaign, 10-20 keywords, 3 ad variations.

**Cost per click by industry:** Legal ($5-50), Home services ($5-25), Healthcare ($3-15), Real estate ($2-10), Restaurants ($1-3).

**Key metric:** Cost per acquisition (CPA). If your CPA is less than 10% of the customer's lifetime value, scale aggressively.

## Content Marketing

Content builds trust over time. It's not fast — but it compounds.

**The 80/20 rule:** 80% educational content (teach, inform, share). 20% promotional (offers, CTAs, sales).

**Blog strategy:** Answer the top 20 questions your customers ask. Each answer becomes a blog post. Each blog post becomes a Google ranking opportunity.

**Content repurposing:** One blog post → 5 social posts → 1 email → 1 video → 1 infographic. Create once, distribute everywhere.

# SECTION 3: INDUSTRY KNOWLEDGE

## Real Estate

**Profit margins:** Commission-based. Typical 2.5-3% per side. Average agent earns $50-80K. Top producers earn $200K+.

**Lead generation:** Zillow leads ($20-50 per lead), Google Ads, open houses, sphere of influence, social media. Referrals have the highest conversion rate (14%) vs. internet leads (1-2%).

**Key metrics:** Leads per month, appointments set, listings taken, homes closed, average commission.

**Growth strategy:** Build a team (ISA for lead follow-up, buyer's agent, listing agent). Focus on a geographic farm area. Send monthly mailers + door knock.

## Restaurants & Food Service

**Profit margins:** Net profit 5-10% (full service), 8-15% (fast casual), 15-25% (food trucks). Food cost should be 28-35% of revenue. Labor should be 25-35%.

**Customer acquisition:** Google Business Profile is #1. Also: Instagram, local events, Yelp, delivery apps (but they take 15-30% commission).

**Key metrics:** Revenue per seat, table turnover rate, food cost percentage, labor cost percentage, average ticket size.

**Growth strategies:** Catering (higher margins), private events, loyalty programs, online ordering (own website, not just third-party apps).

## Construction & Contracting

**Profit margins:** General contractors 8-15%. Subcontractors 10-20%. Specialty trades (electrical, plumbing) 15-25%.

**Lead generation:** Referrals (#1), Google Ads for emergency services, Houzz/Angi for residential, bid boards for commercial. Relationship with property managers and developers.

**Key metrics:** Backlog (months of work booked), gross margin per project, change order rate, customer satisfaction score.

**Pricing:** Cost-plus (cost + markup) or fixed-bid. Always include a contingency (5-10%). Get 50% upfront on residential jobs.

## Healthcare (Dental, Medical, MedSpa)

**Dental profit margins:** 30-40% for general, 40-60% for cosmetic procedures.

**Patient acquisition cost:** $150-300 per new patient. Average patient lifetime value: $3,000-10,000.

**Marketing channels:** Google Ads (#1 for dental), Google Business Profile, patient referral programs, insurance network directories.

**Growth:** Add high-margin services (cosmetic, implants, Invisalign). Hygiene recall programs keep patients coming back every 6 months.

## Legal (Law Firms)

**Profit margins:** Solo practitioners 30-50%. Large firms 25-40%. Contingency fee cases: 33-40% of settlement.

**Client acquisition cost:** $100-500 (family law), $200-1000 (personal injury), $500-5000 (corporate).

**Marketing:** Google Ads (most competitive — $50-200 per click for PI). Avvo, Justia, lawyer directories. Referral networks with other attorneys.

**Key metrics:** Cases opened per month, average case value, collection rate, cost per case acquired.

## Home Services (Plumbing, HVAC, Electrical, Landscaping)

**Profit margins:** Plumbing 15-25%. HVAC 10-20%. Electrical 15-25%. Landscaping 10-20%.

**Lead generation:** Google Ads + Google Business Profile = 70% of leads. Also: Nextdoor, Angi, Thumbtack, yard signs, truck wraps.

**Pricing:** Flat-rate pricing converts better than hourly. Customers prefer knowing the total cost upfront.

**Growth:** Service agreements (monthly/quarterly maintenance contracts). This creates predictable recurring revenue. Upsell during service calls.

## Insurance

**Revenue model:** Commissions (10-20% of premium for P&C, 50-100% first year for life). Renewal commissions create recurring revenue.

**Client acquisition:** Referrals, community involvement, Google Ads for auto/home quotes, partnering with real estate agents and mortgage brokers.

**Key metrics:** Policies per household (bundling increases retention), retention rate (aim for 90%+), loss ratio.

**Growth:** Cross-sell aggressively. Average household has 3.5 insurable needs. Most agents only cover 1.5.

## Financial Services & Mortgage

**Revenue:** Mortgage originators earn 0.5-1% of loan amount. Financial advisors earn 1% AUM annually or flat fees.

**Client acquisition:** LinkedIn for financial advisors. Realtor partnerships for mortgage. Seminars and workshops for both.

**Key metrics:** AUM (assets under management), loans closed per month, average loan size, referral rate.

**Growth:** Centers of influence (CPAs, attorneys, realtors who refer clients). Educational content builds trust in a low-trust industry.

## Fitness & Wellness

**Profit margins:** Boutique fitness 15-25%. Traditional gyms 10-20%. Personal training 40-60%.

**Key metrics:** Member retention rate (average gym loses 30-50% annually), revenue per member, class fill rate.

**Growth:** Reduce churn first — it's cheaper to keep a member than get a new one. Personal check-ins at 30/60/90 days reduce cancellations by 30-40%.

## Beauty (Salons, Barbershops, Spas)

**Profit margins:** 8-15% for salons. Chair rental model can be higher margin but less control.

**Key metrics:** Client retention rate, rebooking rate (should be 60%+), average ticket, revenue per chair per hour.

**Growth:** Upsell services (color with cut, treatment with facial). Rebooking at checkout increases retention by 40%. Loyalty programs work — every 10th visit free.

## E-Commerce & Retail

**Profit margins:** Gross margins 40-60% typical. Net margins 5-15% after advertising and shipping.

**Customer acquisition:** Facebook/Instagram Ads (best for DTC), Google Shopping, email marketing (should be 30-40% of revenue for established stores), influencer partnerships.

**Key metrics:** Customer acquisition cost (CAC), average order value (AOV), customer lifetime value (CLV), return rate, conversion rate (2-3% is average).

**Growth:** Email is your #1 lever. Abandoned cart emails recover 10-15% of lost sales. Post-purchase sequences increase repeat purchases by 25%.

## SaaS & Technology

**Profit margins:** Gross margins 70-85%. Net margins 10-30% (mature). Most startups are unprofitable while growing.

**Key metrics:** MRR (monthly recurring revenue), churn rate (aim for <5% monthly), CAC payback period (should be <12 months), LTV:CAC ratio (should be 3:1+), NRR (net revenue retention, aim for 110%+).

**Growth:** Product-led growth (free trial/freemium) has lowest CAC. Content marketing + SEO for long-term. Sales team for enterprise deals.

## Consulting & Coaching

**Pricing:** Hourly ($150-500/hr), project-based ($5K-50K per project), retainer ($2K-10K/month), value-based (% of results delivered).

**Client acquisition:** LinkedIn thought leadership (#1), speaking, referrals, webinars, podcasting. Cold outreach to warm prospects.

**Key metrics:** Utilization rate (billable hours / available hours — aim for 70%+), average project value, client retention rate.

**Growth:** Move from hourly to value-based pricing. Create a group coaching program (1-to-many). Build intellectual property (frameworks, tools, templates).

## Automotive

**Profit margins:** New car sales 2-5%. Used car sales 10-15%. Service department 40-60% (the real profit center). F&I (finance and insurance) products 100% margin.

**Growth:** Service retention is everything. Customers who service at the dealership are 5x more likely to buy their next car there. Follow-up after every service visit.

## Property Management

**Revenue model:** 8-12% of monthly rent. Also: leasing fees (50-100% of first month's rent), maintenance markup (10-20%), late fees.

**Growth:** Add more doors (properties under management). Target landlords with 2-10 properties — they're overwhelmed but can't afford a full-time property manager. Google Ads for "[city] property management."

**Key metrics:** Doors under management, vacancy rate (aim for <5%), tenant retention rate, maintenance cost per unit, owner retention rate.

# SECTION 4: OPERATIONS

## Hiring Your First Employee

**When to hire:** When you're turning down work or your quality is suffering because you're spread too thin. The cost of NOT hiring (lost revenue) should exceed the cost of hiring.

**First hire by business type:**
- Service businesses: hire a technician/doer (frees you to sell)
- Agencies/consulting: hire an assistant (frees you to deliver)
- Product businesses: hire for fulfillment/operations

**Compensation:** Base salary + performance bonus. For sales roles: base + commission (50/50 split is standard). For service roles: competitive hourly rate + benefits.

**Where to find people:** Indeed, LinkedIn, industry-specific job boards, referrals from your network (best quality), local trade schools.

## Cash Flow Management

**The #1 business killer is running out of cash — not a lack of profit.**

**Rules:**
1. Invoice immediately — not at the end of the month
2. Set net-15 terms, not net-30
3. Require deposits (25-50%) on large projects
4. Build 3 months of operating expenses in reserve
5. Track accounts receivable weekly — chase overdue invoices on day 16

**Cash flow formula:** Starting cash + income received - expenses paid = ending cash. Do this weekly, not monthly.

## Pricing Strategy

**Three approaches:**
1. **Cost-plus:** Calculate costs + add desired profit margin. Simple but leaves money on the table.
2. **Competitive:** Price based on what competitors charge. Race to the bottom.
3. **Value-based:** Price based on the value you deliver to the customer. Best approach for services.

**Raising prices:** Grandfather existing clients for 60-90 days. Raise for all new clients immediately. Most businesses are underpriced by 20-40%. If no one ever pushes back on your prices, you're too cheap.

## Scaling a Business

**Three pillars of scale:**
1. **Systems:** Document every repeatable process. If it depends on you, it doesn't scale.
2. **Team:** Hire for your weaknesses. Your first hire should do the work YOU shouldn't be doing.
3. **Customer acquisition:** Find one channel that works profitably, then scale it before adding new channels.

**The delegation framework:** Only do tasks that require YOUR expertise and generate revenue. Delegate or automate everything else. Your time should be spent on $500/hour activities, not $15/hour ones.

## Business Metrics & KPIs

**Every business should track:**
1. **Revenue** (monthly, quarterly, annual — and trend)
2. **Profit margin** (gross and net)
3. **Customer acquisition cost** (total marketing spend / new customers)
4. **Customer lifetime value** (average revenue per customer × average relationship length)
5. **Cash in bank** (weekly)

**The golden ratio:** LTV should be 3x or more of CAC. If it's less, you're spending too much to acquire customers or not retaining them long enough.

# SECTION 5: FINANCE

## Business Entity Structures

**Sole Proprietor:** Simplest. No liability protection. File on personal taxes (Schedule C). Good for testing a business idea.

**LLC:** Liability protection. Flexible taxation. Easy to set up ($50-500 depending on state). Best for most small businesses.

**S-Corp:** LLC that elects S-Corp taxation. Saves on self-employment tax once profit exceeds ~$50K. You pay yourself a reasonable salary, take remaining profit as distributions (no FICA tax).

**C-Corp:** Separate tax entity. Double taxation (corporate + personal). Needed if you want VC funding or plan to go public. Not for most small businesses.

**Recommendation:** Start as LLC. Elect S-Corp status once you're consistently profiting $50K+/year. Talk to a CPA.

## Small Business Tax Deductions

**Commonly missed deductions:**
- Home office (simplified: $5/sq ft, up to 300 sq ft = $1,500)
- Vehicle mileage (67 cents per mile in 2024)
- Health insurance premiums (100% deductible for self-employed)
- Retirement contributions (SEP IRA: up to 25% of net income)
- Business meals (50% deductible)
- Professional development and courses
- Software and subscriptions
- Cell phone and internet (business portion)
- Marketing and advertising expenses

**Quarterly estimated taxes:** If you expect to owe $1,000+ in taxes, pay quarterly (April 15, June 15, Sept 15, Jan 15). Underpayment penalties add up.

## Business Funding Options

1. **Bootstrapping:** Use revenue to fund growth. Slowest but you keep 100% ownership.
2. **SBA loans:** 7(a) loans up to $5M. Lower rates than bank loans. Requires good credit (680+) and 2+ years in business.
3. **Business line of credit:** Flexible. Draw as needed. Good for cash flow gaps.
4. **Revenue-based financing:** Repay as a % of revenue. Good for seasonal businesses.
5. **Angel investors:** $25K-500K. Give up 10-25% equity. Best for high-growth businesses.
6. **VC funding:** $500K-50M+. Give up 20-40% equity per round. Only for businesses targeting $100M+ markets.

**Rule of thumb:** If your business can grow profitably without outside money, don't take it. Debt is better than equity — you can pay off a loan, but you can never get equity back.

## Exit Strategy & Business Valuation

**Valuation methods:**
- **Revenue multiple:** SaaS = 5-15x ARR. Service businesses = 1-3x revenue. Retail = 0.5-2x revenue.
- **EBITDA multiple:** Most small businesses = 3-5x EBITDA. Well-run businesses with systems = 5-8x.
- **Discretionary earnings:** SDE (seller's discretionary earnings) × multiple. Most common for businesses under $5M.

**What increases value:** Recurring revenue, systems that don't depend on the owner, diversified customer base (no single client = 20%+ of revenue), documented processes, strong team.

**What decreases value:** Owner-dependent (you ARE the business), declining revenue, customer concentration, no systems, legal/compliance issues.
